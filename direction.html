03 · The Accent Decision

Clay Is the Default. Here's Why.

Both briefs' candidates, side by side. Switching later is a one-variable change (--clay) — nothing else on the page moves.

Chosen · Muted Clay #9C4A2B

You Have a Leverage Problem.

Warm, grounded, confident — and ownable.

Clay sits naturally beside bone and brass, photographs of women in warm light, and espresso ink. Crucially: your closest competitor runs purple-on-cream, so clay gives you a lane nobody in the niche owns.

Alternative · Deep Plum #5E3148

You Have a Leverage Problem.

Elegant, but it concedes territory.

Plum reads premium and passes every contrast check — but it drifts toward the purple-on-cream template look your competitor already occupies, and it cools the page down. Available on request as a one-line swap.

04 · Logo Concepts

Three Marks. One Recommendation.

All single-color, monoline, legible at 32px, no gradients or fine detail. Each shown on bone and on espresso.

Recommended A · The Rise Loop

A loop that refuses to close — the cycle of repurposing breaking upward into a rise. The whole business in one stroke, and it's abstract enough to trademark cleanly.

Use: favicon · profile picture · content watermark · everywhere
B · The Signal

One source radiating outward and upward — one episode reaching every platform. Strong as a repeating pattern element, but at tiny sizes it can read as a Wi-Fi glyph.

Use: section accents · carousel pattern · packaging, not favicon
C · The Ampersand Seal

The "&" is the hinge of your name — Fraunces' italic ampersand in a hairline ring reads like a wax seal. Elegant, but it depends on the typeface: outline it in a vector tool before any trademark filing.

Use: podcast cover corner · email signature · social profile on dark
